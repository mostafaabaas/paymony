Trust rules

The rules every finance video follows

Finance is a regulated surface. These five rules are not a style choice — they are the conditions under which we make content at all.

✓

No promised returns. No video states or implies earnings, profits or outcomes. If a claim can’t be evidenced, it doesn’t get said.

✓

Approved wording only. Product claims come from your compliance-approved copy — never from a creator’s improvisation.

✓

Test accounts on camera. Every demo uses test or demo accounts. Real balances, cards and credentials are never filmed.

✓

Review before publish. Nothing goes live until your team has seen the final cut and signed it off.

✓

Disclosed partnerships. Paid content is labelled as paid — on every platform, in every format.

Trust

Finance-app trust rules

Trust in finance content is a system, not a tone of voice. These are the rules we run it by:

Show the product, not the outcome

A demo can show how a transfer works. It cannot show what the transfer will earn. The line is bright: process is content, outcomes are promises.

One source of claims

Every factual claim traces back to a document your compliance team approved. If it isn’t in that document, the creator doesn’t say it.

Disclose, always

Paid partnerships are labelled in the content itself. Disclosure isn’t a legal footnote — it’s the reason finance audiences keep watching.

Arab markets

Fintech content across Arab markets: the Gulf and Morocco don't read the same

A finance app expanding across the Arab world faces one product and several different audiences. The content system that respects that looks like this:

Language is per market, not per region

Gulf audiences take finance content in their own Arabic dialect. Morocco reads in Darija mixed with French — product terms included. Commission each market's cut from creators of that market; a single video dubbed for "the Arab world" convinces no one, least of all about money.

Trust signals are local

What reassures a user differs by market: the licence and regulator references your compliance team approves there, the payment habits people actually have, and the questions support gets asked. Build each market's claims document from its own approved facts — never copy one market's trust claims into another's script.

One compliance core, local editions

The discipline stays identical everywhere: no promised returns, no outcome claims, disclosure inside the content. What changes is the edition — script, creator, dialect and examples per market, reviewed against that market's claims document before anything publishes.

Safe demos

Demoing a payment app safely

A payment app demo films the most sensitive screen in a user’s life. This is how we keep it safe:

Test accounts, staged data

Demos use sandbox accounts with staged balances and dummy contacts. Nothing real is ever one screenshot away from the camera.

Block the leaks

Notifications off, recent apps cleared — no real OTP, PIN or card number is ever typed or shown. The flow is real; the data is not.

Review the frames, not just the script

Compliance reviews the final cut frame by frame, because the risky moment is usually a background screen nobody scripted.

Creator briefs

Briefing creators for fintech

A fintech creator brief has two jobs: keep the creator interesting, and keep the claims exact. Ours does both — by separating the two:

Fixed claims, free delivery

Product facts and required wording arrive locked. Everything else — hook, story, style — is the creator’s. That is the whole deal.

The claims checklist

A one-page list of what can be said, what can’t, and the exact approved phrases — signed by the creator before shooting.

Review is a step, not a surprise

The brief names the review step and its turnaround up front, so creators plan for compliance instead of colliding with it.

Saudi fintech

UGC for Saudi Fintech Apps: Content That Builds Trust and Explains the Product

Saudi users adopt the finance apps they understand — and they understand the ones they have seen real people use. That is the whole case for UGC in Saudi fintech: a creator walking through the real app, in plain language, answers the trust question before it becomes a support ticket or an abandoned signup. The formats, and the discipline that makes them work:

Formats that teach, not hype

App walkthroughs that show the real flow screen by screen; onboarding explainers that take a new user from signup to first use; creator demos built on real use cases — paying, transferring, topping up; and FAQ-style short videos that answer the questions support hears every week. Every format demonstrates the product; none of them performs a lifestyle around it.

Compliance care is built into the format

Finance content explains; it never promises returns and never gives financial advice. Claims come only from the brand's own approved product documentation, demos run on test accounts, paid partnerships are disclosed in the content, and campaigns respect the local advertising rules that apply to financial services in the market. A video that needs a promise to work is the wrong video.

From brief to measurement

The workflow runs in order: a written brief carrying the approved claims list, creator selection matched to the Saudi audience the product serves, the brand's compliance review of the final cut, then publish. Measure what shows understanding, not just attention: installs, completed onboarding, and saves and shares — the signals that a viewer understood the product and intends to return to it.

Questions Saudi fintech teams ask

Why does UGC work for Saudi fintech apps specifically?

Because the barrier is trust, not awareness. A Saudi user deciding whether to move money through a new app wants to see it used by a real person, in their language, before they commit. Creator demos answer that in a way a product film cannot — provided the claims stay inside the brand's approved wording.

Can a creator explain fees and product terms?

Yes — explanation is the job. A creator can walk through how a fee or a term works using the brand's published, compliance-approved wording. What a creator cannot do is promise an outcome, project a return, or advise viewers on what to do with their money.

Who reviews the content before it publishes?

The brand's own compliance team. Every final cut is reviewed and signed off before anything goes live, and the paid partnership is disclosed in the content itself. That review step is scheduled in the brief, not added at the end.

FAQ

What fintech marketers ask first

Straight answers, before the first call.

Can creators promise results if our product performs well?

No. Content can demonstrate how a product works and what it does; it never promises earnings, returns or outcomes — for any product, however it performs. That rule protects your licence and your users.

Who approves content before it publishes?

Your team. Every final cut goes through your compliance review and publishes only after sign-off. Nothing goes live on a creator’s word alone.

How do demos avoid showing real customer data?

All demos are filmed on test or sandbox accounts with staged data. Real balances, OTPs, PINs and card numbers are never typed, shown or spoken on camera.

Are paid posts disclosed?

Yes — every paid placement is labelled as a partnership in the content itself, on every platform. Undisclosed paid finance content is not something we produce.

Is Paymony a financial service?

No. Paymony is a content and creator service of influUGC. We don’t provide financial advice, brokerage or payment services — we produce content for the licensed companies that do.
