Guide 1 · Trust rules

UGC for finance apps — the trust rules

Finance is the one category where a creator's enthusiasm can become the brand's liability. A video that overpromises does not just perform badly — it misleads people about money. These are the rules every finance campaign we run is built on:

  • Never promise returns, profits or outcomes. No "earn", "grow your money", "guaranteed", or any phrasing that tells a viewer what will happen to their money. A creator can show what the app does; they cannot tell anyone what the app will do for them.
  • Demonstrate, don't project. Honest demos show real flows: opening the app, making a payment, finding a fee, reading a statement. The moment a demo turns into a projection — charts going up, balances multiplying — it has left marketing and entered fiction.
  • Risk language in plain words. Where a product carries risk, fees or conditions, the content says so in language a first-time user understands — not whispered, not in fine print added later. If the product team is uncomfortable with a sentence, the sentence does not ship.
  • Claims come from documents, not enthusiasm. Fees, eligibility, limits, licences — a creator may only state what the brand's published product documentation says, worded the way the brand's compliance team approved it.
  • Disclose the partnership. Paid content is marked as paid, using the platform's disclosure tools and clear wording. Trust content that hides that it is advertising destroys the exact asset it was meant to build.
  • One accountable reviewer. Every video passes the brand's compliance or legal review before publishing. "The creator posted it already" is not a review process.

None of this makes content weaker. In finance, the brands people trust are the ones whose content sounds like their compliance team was in the room — because for the good ones, it was.

Guide 2 · Safe demos

How to demo a payment app on camera without exposing real money or data

A screen recording of a finance app is a privacy incident waiting for one careless frame. Balances, account numbers, one-time codes, a notification sliding in from a real conversation — any of them can end up in a published video. The protocol we film by:

Before filming

  • Use a test, demo or sandbox account wherever the product provides one. If it does not, the brand creates a dedicated filming account with staged values — a creator's real account with real funds is never the set.
  • Stage the values. Balances and transfer amounts on screen are demo figures chosen for the video. They exist to show the flow, not to impress — and never presented as earnings or results.
  • Silence the phone. Notifications off, do-not-disturb on, for the whole session. One message preview can expose a name, a number or a code that no edit was meant to catch.
  • Clean the frame. Recent transactions, saved beneficiaries, chat lists, photo galleries in shared sheets — anything personal is closed or hidden before recording starts.

Never on camera, no exceptions

OTPs and verification codes, PINs, full card numbers, IBANs and account numbers, QR codes tied to real accounts, and real customer data of any kind. If a flow needs a code, the video cuts around the code step or uses the demo environment's test code shown as a product behaviour, never a real credential. Anything doubtful that slipped into a take is blurred or the take is re-shot — the frame is checked before delivery, not after publishing.

After filming

Raw footage of finance demos is handled like the data it shows: shared only inside the production chain, reviewed by the brand before publishing, and the staged accounts' credentials are rotated or closed when the campaign ends. A demo account left open with a shared password is a loose end with a balance attached.

Guide 3 · Briefing

Creator briefs for fintech — the compliance checklist

In fintech, the brief is a compliance document that happens to produce videos. If a control is not in the brief, it does not exist on filming day. Here is the checklist structure we brief from:

The approved claims list

One page, supplied by the brand, listing the only product facts a creator may state: what the product is, who it is for, fees and limits as published, the licence or regulatory status worded exactly as the brand's compliance team approved it. Anything not on the list — a feature coming soon, a comparison with a competitor, a number from a news article — is not said.

The never-say list

  • No promised returns, profits, earnings or "guaranteed" anything
  • No pressure language — no false urgency about money decisions
  • No personal-finance advice dressed as content ("you should put your savings here")
  • No claims about competitors, named or implied
  • No real credentials, codes or customer data on screen (Guide 2)

Disclosure, written in

The brief states how disclosure appears: the platform's paid-partnership label plus clear wording in the content, placed where a viewer sees it before the message, not after. The brief also states the market and platform rules the campaign follows, as the brand's team specifies them.

The approval gate

The brief names the reviewer and the rule: draft → brand compliance review → corrections → publish, in that order, every time — including stories and short formats, which are where undisciplined lines usually escape. Usage scope (where the video may run, paid use, duration) is agreed in the same document, so the legal life of the video is settled before it exists.

06 — FAQ

What fintech marketers ask before their first pack

Who owns the videos — can we use them on our channels and in our ads?

Usage is agreed in writing before production starts. The brief and quote spell out where the videos may be used — your channels, your paid ad accounts, your in-app placements — on which platforms, and for how long. The usage scope you buy is the usage scope written in your agreement.

Can creators promise results, returns or profits for our product?

No — never. It is a hard rule on every fintech campaign we run: no promised returns, no profit claims, no guaranteed outcomes of any kind. Creators demonstrate how the product works and describe their experience of using it; anything that sounds like a promise of a financial result is cut before publishing, and the brand's approved claims list defines the only product facts a creator may state.

How do you film an app demo without exposing real money or personal data?

Demos are filmed on test, demo or sandbox accounts wherever the product provides them — never on a creator's real account with real funds. Balances and amounts on screen are staged demo values, notifications are silenced during filming, and OTPs, PINs, card numbers, IBANs and QR codes for real accounts never appear. Guide 2 above explains the full protocol.

Who checks the content before it goes live?

Every fintech video passes the brand's own compliance or legal review before publishing — that step is built into the production plan, not added at the end. Nothing goes live unapproved: the review checks the claims list, the risk language, the disclosure and the demo protocol, and flagged lines are re-shot or cut.

Do creators have to disclose that the content is paid?

Yes. Disclosure is written into every brief: the creator marks the content as a paid partnership using the platform's disclosure tools and clear wording in the content itself, following the rules of the platform and the market the campaign runs in. Undisclosed paid content is a breach of the brief.

Are prices published?

No prices are published. Packs are structured — a defined number of videos, creators and usage scope — and priced in a written quote after a free consultation or brief, based on the creators, videos and usage you need.
